How to play Inky Keys

Ink creatures crawl across a notebook page carrying letters and words. Type what each one carries before it reaches the red margin on the left. Every creature that gets through drains your ink well, and the page is over when it runs dry.

The basics

  • Type a creature's letter or word to blot it up. The one nearest the margin is picked first, and Tab switches to the next.
  • Correct keys in a row build a score multiplier, up to ×4. A wrong key costs a few points and breaks the run.
  • Pages and chapters: a notebook is a series of chapters, each a few pages long. Pages speed up a little as you go, and finishing a chapter tops up your ink.
  • Esc pauses. The game also pauses by itself if you switch to another window.

Ways to play

Free Write

Pick exactly which keys you want to practice in the Type Case, a keyboard where you click keys on or off (or start from a preset like Home row, All letters or Numbers). The game builds every page from only those keys: single letters on drips, and real words that use just those letters on the word pages. Choose Relaxed, Normal, Hard or Expert.

Apprentice

A 27-chapter typing course for beginners. Chapter 1 teaches just f and j, the keys with bumps, and each chapter after that adds one new key: the rest of the home row, then space, then the other letters. Every chapter opens with a card showing the new key and which finger types it. Pages earn up to three stars.

The Daily Page

One short seeded notebook a day, the same for everyone on the same keyboard layout, so you can compare scores with friends and family. Only your first go counts, and streaks build day by day.

Fair Copy

A typing-speed test with no creatures. Copy out a passage from a classic book, such as Pride and Prejudice or A Tale of Two Cities, or type as much as you can in 30 or 60 seconds (press Enter at the ↵ mark to go on to the next passage). The clock starts on your first key. At the end you see your words per minute (WPM), accuracy and slowest keys. Mistakes you leave in cost speed, so fix them with Backspace as you go. Fair Copy remembers your best speed for each test and has its own shelf of stickers.

The creatures

DripsA single character.
ScrawlersA word, written on their belly.
Run-onsA whole phrase on a centipede. Mistakes stay struck through in red until you fix them with Backspace.
Crimson and violetTougher ink: crimson ones need two words in a row, violet ones three. Dots show how many are left.
GhostsInvisible ink: their text only shows once they are part of the way across the page.

Powers

  • Charges: a long run of correct keys earns a charge, shown as drops next to the ink bar.
  • The Blotter: press Enter to spend charges and blot every creature on the page at once.
  • The Eraser forgives a wrong key, and a second of quick slips after it.

Bosses

The last page of each chapter starts with a boss. They take turns:

  • The Spill, a big, slow word that splits into small Inkmites.
  • The Bookworm, a chain of short words where only the head can be typed.
  • The Shuffler, whose untyped letters swap places every few seconds.
  • The Inkwell Squid, guarded by tentacle words that squirt ink over the page.
  • The Rival Quill, a pen that races you to write the same word.

Progress and rewards

  • Every page is graded in red pen, from A+ to F, and circles the keys you missed most.
  • Pages earn ink, which raises your level and rank and unlocks new pens, ink colors and paper for your desk.
  • A sticker book collects achievements, and each key fills with ink in the Type Case as you master it.
  • Several players can share one browser, each with their own progress.

Keyboards and comfort

Inky Keys supports US QWERTY, Dvorak, Colemak, French AZERTY and Spanish keyboards. Menus and words can each be in English or French (en français); French words keep their accents, and the Apprentice contents gain five accent chapters that show the keys for each one. Settings offer larger text, high-contrast and night colors, reduced motion and sound controls.
