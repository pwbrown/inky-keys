For parents and teachers

Inky Keys is a free typing game made by a parent who wanted a gentle way for their own kids to learn to type. It runs in any modern web browser, on a computer with a keyboard.

What it teaches

  • Touch typing, one key at a time. The Apprentice course starts with f and j on the home row and adds a single new key per chapter, showing which finger types it on a picture of two hands.
  • Only the keys a child knows. Every word and letter on the page is built from the keys they have learned so far, so they are never asked to type something they haven't been taught.
  • French, with its accents. Menus and words can each be English or French. French words keep their accents, and five accent chapters show the keys for each one, on a French AZERTY keyboard or a US one (Mac, Windows or Chromebook). A Forgiving accents setting lets e count for é for a child who finds them too hard; Daily Page results note when it was on.
  • Their weak spots. The game keeps per-key accuracy and speed, leans practice toward the keys that need it, and circles the most-missed keys on each graded page.
  • Speed and accuracy. Fair Copy is a calm typing test with no creatures: copy out a passage from a classic book, or type for 30 or 60 seconds, and see words per minute, accuracy and the slowest keys. Leaving mistakes in costs speed, so it rewards careful typing.
  • Practice on your terms. In Free Write you choose the exact keys: just the home row, numbers, capitals, or the symbols used in code.

Safe by design

  • No ads, no accounts, no tracking. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to buy.
  • Progress stays on the device. Saves live in the browser and are never uploaded. You can back them up to a file.
  • Family-friendly words. Swear words, slurs and words that don't suit young children are always left out.
  • Works offline after the first visit, and can be installed like an app.
  • Open source. Anyone can read exactly what it does. See the privacy page for the details.

At home and in class

  • Several players per browser. Siblings or a class sharing a computer each get their own name, progress and stickers.
  • The Daily Page is the same short notebook for everyone each day, which makes a friendly daily warm-up or a class challenge.
  • Gentle difficulty. Relaxed mode and the Apprentice course's slow early chapters suit beginners; Hard and Expert keep fast typists busy.
  • Accessible. A highly legible font, larger text sizes, high-contrast and night colors, reduced motion, and menus you can use with the keyboard alone.
  • Moving devices. A transfer link carries a player's progress to another computer, with nothing sent to any server.

Getting started

Open inkykeys.com, choose Apprentice for a complete beginner or Free Write to pick keys, and press Enter. Ten minutes a day goes a long way.
